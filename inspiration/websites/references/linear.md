# Linear

**URL:** https://linear.app · **Category:** project tracking / developer-tool SaaS · **Industry:** software · **Provenance:** canonical knowledge, recorded 2026-11-25 · **Verify live:** current brand refresh state, exact typefaces, new sections

## Why it is worth studying

Linear defined the "dark developer-tool aesthetic" of the 2020s — and the reason it keeps being cited is that its dark theme is *systemic*, not cosmetic: contrast, elevation and motion all behave coherently on dark. It is also the reference for the "product IS the marketing" school: the landing page shows the actual interface at real size, doing real work.

## Visual direction

Dark, precise, product-led minimalism. Deep near-black surfaces, restrained accents, glow/elevation used to separate layers (the dark-mode equivalent of whitespace). No ornament that the product itself does not justify.

## Typography

Inter-class grotesque with disciplined sizing; tight tracking on display sizes; mono accents for anything technical. The type's job is clarity at speed — Linear's brand promise rendered typographically.

## Colour system

Layered dark surfaces (the dark-theme token architecture in practice: [../../colour/colour-in-code.md](../../../colour/colour-in-code.md)) with one restrained accent for interactive emphasis; semantic states (issue statuses) are a mini colour system worth studying for any status-heavy UI.

## Composition and layout

Tight, centred product surfaces; section rhythm alternates promise → product proof → social proof with almost no filler. Density is deliberately higher than consumer SaaS — the audience is practitioners.

## Navigation

Minimal top nav; the product's own information architecture does most of the wayfinding on page. Keyboard-first ethos visible even in marketing surfaces.

## Interesting sections

- **Hero:** the product at real size, live-feeling, with one motion moment (the entrance choreography).
- **Feature reveals:** short sections, one idea each — a model of "one section per screen".
- **Changelog as marketing:** the product's velocity is the social proof — a content pattern worth stealing for any fast-moving tool.
- **Pricing:** clear tiers, product-anchored.

## Interaction and motion

The signature: smooth, short, origin-aware motion everywhere — modals from their triggers, state changes that explain themselves. Linear's motion is the reason "it feels expensive" gets attributed to animation quality ([../../motion-and-interaction/motion-principles.md](../../../motion-and-interaction/motion-principles.md)).

## Imagery and visual assets

The product itself, rendered at high fidelity (screenshots and interface recreations), plus abstract 3D/glow accents. Photography: nearly none.

## Distinctive details

Dark-mode elevation logic; the issue-status colour semantics; the entrance choreography; the changelog voice (copy as craft — [../../skills/taste/SKILL.md](../../../skills/taste/SKILL.md) copy rules in the wild).

## What makes it effective

Credibility with practitioners. Every surface signals "built by people who care about software craft", which is the purchase argument for the tool.

## What to learn (and what to leave)

**Learn:** dark-mode as a system (not a theme toggle); product-at-real-size marketing; motion as craft signal; changelog-as-marketing; status-colour semantics. **Leave:** the dark-first assumption (it is a product-audience decision, not a fashion default), and the extremely tight density without the typography to carry it.

## Suitable project types

Developer tools, practitioner-facing SaaS, dark-theme products, anything status/velocity-driven.
