# Ramp

**URL:** https://ramp.com · **Category:** spend management / fintech · **Industry:** finance (B2B) · **Provenance:** canonical knowledge, recorded 2026-11-25 · **Verify live:** current brand era, section set

## Why it is worth studying

Ramp is the reference for *editorial fintech*: a brand that borrows magazine typography (serif-accented pairing, generous editorial layouts) and data-rich trust surfaces to make a finance product feel cultured and precise. For any product that must communicate numbers *and* taste — the whole B2B fintech category — it is the study object for typography-led authority.

## Visual direction

Editorial-modern B2B: restrained palette, serif-accented display type, data visualisations as brand assets, photography treated editorially. The position: "we are serious money people with taste".

## Typography

The signature move is the **serif-accent pairing** — a serif display face carrying headlines against a grotesque workhorse ([../../typography/font-pairing.md](../../typography/font-pairing.md) contrast pairing), with tabular numerals honoured in every data surface. Editorial measure and leading discipline on longer content.

## Colour system

Restrained: a calm neutral base, one or two deep accents, semantic colours functional in data surfaces. The restraint is the trust mechanism — the opposite failure is fintech neon ([../../accessibility/accessible-colour.md](../../accessibility/accessible-colour.md) and token discipline apply).

## Composition and layout

Editorial layouts: asymmetric feature spreads, pull-quote moments, generous margins; data sections use disciplined card/tabular composition ([../../frontend-engineering/forms-and-data-display.md](../../frontend-engineering/forms-and-data-display.md) dashboard anatomy).

## Navigation

Clear B2B nav (product lines, solutions, resources); the resources/blog surfaces keep the editorial voice — content marketing that does not feel like content marketing.

## Interesting sections

- **Editorial hero:** serif headline over composed photography — the authority-first first screen.
- **Data/trust surfaces:** savings numbers presented with chart craft ([../../information-design/data-visualisation.md](../../information-design/data-visualisation.md)) — the numbers ARE the marketing.
- **Case-study surfaces:** customer stories laid out like magazine features (the B2B social-proof reference).
- **Product surfaces (dashboard marketing):** the real product rendered in the editorial system — the seam worth studying.

## Interaction and motion

Quiet: editorial reveals, subtle chart animations (numbers that count truthfully), hover states in the serif/grotesque system. Motion stays subordinate to the voice.

## Imagery and visual assets

Editorial photography (treated, consistent grading — [../../imagery/image-treatment.md](../../imagery/image-treatment.md)), real data visualisations, product UI. No decorative illustration.

## Distinctive details

The serif/grotesque pairing; numerals discipline; editorial grading on photography; the chart craft; case studies as magazine features.

## What makes it effective

Authority with taste: the typography says "we are serious", the data says "we are real", and the editorial voice differentiates in a category of template SaaS.

## What to learn (and what to leave)

**Learn:** serif-accent pairing for authority; numbers as marketing with real chart craft; editorial layouts for B2B; photography grading consistency. **Leave:** the serif authority for playful consumer products, and editorial pacing when the audience needs speed.

## Suitable project types

B2B fintech, professional services, data products, content-heavy B2B.
